# ComplaintMe AI — Project Principles

> The constitution of ComplaintMe AI. Every ticket, feature, and line of code is
> accountable to this document.

**ComplaintMe AI exists to transform customer complaints into business intelligence that drives better decisions.**

---

## Vision

A world where no customer complaint is ever wasted.

Every complaint is a free, unsolicited signal about product quality, service
gaps, and operational risk. Most organizations bury these signals in ticket
queues. ComplaintMe AI turns them into decisions.

## Mission

Give any organization — from a single support agent to an enterprise quality
team — an intelligence engine that reads complaints the way a seasoned analyst
would: understanding what happened, recognizing what has happened before,
measuring whether it is getting worse, and recommending what to do about it.

## Product Philosophy

Instead of asking users to fill complicated complaint forms, we ask only one
question:

> **Tell us what happened.**

Everything else — category, company, product, severity, sentiment, department,
priority, business impact, recommended action — is generated by Cortexa, the
platform's intelligence engine. The human provides the story; the AI provides
the structure and the judgment.

## The Golden Question

Before implementing any feature, ask:

> **Does this help someone make a better decision?**

If the answer is no, reconsider the feature.

ComplaintMe AI succeeds by improving decision quality, not by increasing
feature count.

## Market Gap

Traditional complaint management systems are **record-keeping systems**. They
store complaints, route tickets, and track SLAs. The intelligence — spotting a
pattern, judging risk, deciding to escalate — is left entirely to humans
reading queues.

ComplaintMe AI is **not a traditional complaint management system**. It is a
**decision-support intelligence platform** that happens to ingest complaints:

| Traditional CMS | ComplaintMe AI |
| --- | --- |
| Stores what the customer said | Understands what the customer meant |
| Shows a list of tickets | Shows patterns, trends, and risk |
| Humans triage every item | Cortexa triages; humans decide |
| Value = tickets closed | Value = decisions improved |
| Forms with 15 required fields | One question: *tell us what happened* |

No mainstream product today answers, automatically and per complaint:
*Has this happened before? Is it getting worse? Should someone act?*
That gap is the product.

## Development Principles

1. **Never break existing functionality.** Every change is additive or
   backward-compatible. Existing API consumers must never notice a release.
2. **Modular architecture first.** Routers, services, repositories, and AI
   providers are separate layers with explicit contracts. No layer reaches
   around another.
3. **AI providers must remain swappable.** All intelligence flows through the
   `CortexaProvider` contract. Replacing the heuristic engine with a hosted
   LLM must never require changes to services, APIs, or the frontend.
4. **Intelligence over complexity.** A deterministic rule that answers the
   business question today beats a sophisticated model that ships next
   quarter. Upgrade the internals later — behind the same contract.
5. **Business value over feature count.** Apply the Golden Question. Features
   that don't improve a decision don't ship.
6. **Privacy first.** Complaints are customer data. No complaint content
   leaves the platform boundary without an explicit, configured decision
   (e.g., opting into a hosted LLM provider).
7. **Enterprise ready.** Multi-tenant-shaped data model (`organization_id`
   from day one), soft deletes, audit-friendly status history, and additive
   migrations.

## UI Philosophy

The interface should feel like a **premium consumer product**, not an
enterprise back office.

**Inspired by:** Apple, Linear, Stripe, Vercel, Arc, ChatGPT — calm surfaces,
generous whitespace, purposeful motion, one clear action per screen, and
intelligence presented as a finished thought rather than a data dump.

**Explicitly not:** traditional CRM screens, government portals, legacy
ticketing software — dense grids, modal mazes, and forms that make the user do
the machine's work.

Practical rules:

- Never show an empty card; show an elegant "Cortexa is analyzing…" state.
- Present AI output as executive-ready narrative, not raw fields.
- Motion communicates state (analyzing → completed), never decoration alone.
- Every screen should be understandable by a first-time user without training.
